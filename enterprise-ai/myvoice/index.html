/myvoice
write it like me, not like an AI

A ghostwriting skill built from one person's real messages, three independent AI analysts, and one rule: nothing goes in the standard unless all three agreed and the corpus proved it.

Every model has a default voice. It is smooth, balanced, and it sounds like nobody. Post it under your own name and people can tell in one sentence.

/myvoice fixes that at the source. It measures how one human actually writes, turns the measurements into rules with real counts, and makes the assistant pass a litmus test before anything ships.

01Why it exists

  
The order came in at two in the morning, after one too many posts that read like a press release.

  

    

      
The ask, verbatim

      
"Use OUR CLI agents to help come up with a full analysis of my writing style and speech patterns. Minus my typos. Then use that CLI and your analysis to form the basis of the rewrite and all future socials posts."

      
"Make sure they do a proper deep-research audit. Not just skimming."

      
"Consensus must be reached among all."

    

    

      
What that rules out

      
No vibes. No "sound casual" prompt. No single model's opinion of what the person sounds like. The standard had to be measured, cross-checked by independent analysts, and every rule had to carry a number from the full corpus. Typos are noise, not style. Voice, never spelling.

03How it was built

  
A pipeline, not a prompt. Each stage has an artifact you can open.

  

    

      
Stage 1
Corpus

      
2,004 messages pulled from session archives, verbatim, typos kept so nothing is edited by hand. Separated by a delimiter so a model can read them as a list.

    

    

    

      
Stage 2
Three analysts

      
The same forensic prompt sent to three different CLI agents through one wrapper. Each wrote its own style profile without seeing the others.

      
grokcodexantigravity+ statistical profile

    

    

    

      
Stage 3
Consensus + count

      
A claim becomes a rule only if all three analysts made it and a grep over the full corpus backs it with a real frequency. Claims that failed the count were rejected and recorded as rejected.

    

    

    

      
Stage 4
Standard + skill

      
The rules live in one README with the counts beside them. The skill is the applicator: it reads the standard on every run, drafts, then runs the litmus test.

    

  






  
04The prompt the analysts got

  
Same text to every analyst. Seven numbered sections so the outputs line up for comparison. Quotes required as evidence. No flattery allowed.

  
TASK: Writing-style forensic analysis of one person, from his real chat messages below.

You are analyzing the verbatim messages of one human (separator: ---). They were typed fast on phones and keyboards, so IGNORE typos and misspellings entirely. They are noise, not style. Analyze the underlying VOICE.

Deliver a structured STYLE PROFILE with these exact sections:
1. SENTENCE RHYTHM      typical length, cadence, how he chains thoughts
2. OPENERS & CONNECTORS how he starts messages, favorite transitions
3. VOCABULARY & REGISTER plain vs technical, characteristic phrases (quote real examples), what he NEVER says
4. TONE MARKERS          how enthusiasm, frustration, skepticism and humor actually show up (quote)
5. THINKING PATTERN      how he frames problems, decisions, asks
6. GHOSTWRITING RULES    10 to 15 concrete do/don't rules for posts that sound like HIM, with AI-isms to ban
7. LITMUS TEST           5 quick checks to run on any draft

Be concrete. Quote his actual phrases as evidence. No flattery, no padding. Output markdown only.

05What is in it: the twelve Tier 1 rules

  
Each one is law because all three analysts said it and the corpus count agreed. The counts are real.

  

    
Short, load-bearing sentences. One job each.
Fragments are legal and characteristic. "Same model. Same set up."
avg 21.4 words / message

    
Start mid-conversation, never with a thesis.
Openers are "Okay", "I", a question, "Hey", "No/Yes", "Also", "So".
"Okay" 188× · "Hey" 79×

    
"Also" is the semicolon.
New requirements bolt on with Also / And / But. Nothing nests in a subclause.
"Also" 199×

    
Write the next physical action, not the concept.
"Make sure", "lets get", "lets". Verbs that move something.
"make sure" 110× · "lets" 89×

    
Questions drive.
Often a command wearing a question mark. "Wanna try a 1 minute sustained test?"
29% of messages ask one

    
Zero emoji. No hashtag walls.
Three emoji in two thousand messages. Emoji bullets and podiums are pure AI cologne.
0.15% base rate

    
The AI-word ban.
Furthermore, delve, tapestry, testament, foster, moreover, leverage, robust, seamless, holistic, paradigm, game-changer. Also em-dash chains, "Here's the thing", tricolon flourishes.
~0 occurrences in corpus

    
Plain-speak and deep jargon in one breath.
"Tensor parallel" and "give me the real poop" in the same sentence. Never corporate-neutral.
no middle register found

    
Physical verbs for digital things.
"Make the GPU sing", "bake off", "full send", "buttoned up".
"sing" 80×

    
Emotion in words, not punctuation.
Enthusiasm is a CAPS burst and visceral slang. Frustration is profanity and a direct question.
15% CAPS bursts · 0.06 ! per msg

    
Outcome first, cost aware.
What it does, what it costs in time, what is next. "It took 45 minutes last time."
numbers beat adjectives

    
Ownership voice.
My lab, my machines, and the machines have names. First person, possessive, no passive voice.
every node named

Tier 2: season, don't saturate
Hard interrupts like "Wait / STOP / Before" when stakes are high. Numbered lists only when answering someone else's menu. Profanity is 15% in private chat and dials to zero-to-mild in public.

    
Rejected after verification
Heavy exclamation points (0.06 per message). Emoji bullets. Formal transitions and topic sentences. All three were tempting, none survived the count.

    
One branded exception
For published social posts only: the bee badge may close the post and two to four plain hashtags may ride along for reach. That is branding, not voice. Everything else in the emoji rule stands.

  






  
07What the skill does when you call it

  
The skill is the applicator. The standard is the law. If they ever disagree, the standard wins.

  

    
1. Load
Reads the standard fresh on every invocation. Rules, seasonings, ban list, litmus. Never from memory, so an edit to the standard is live on the next call.

    
2. Draft
Writes in the voice: short sentences, mid-conversation opener, Also as the connector, numbers over adjectives, physical verbs, machines by name, zero emoji, emotion in words.

    
3. Register check
Same voice, different dial per destination. LinkedIn: profanity to zero or mild. X: one mild if it earns it. Internal chat: full natural register.

    
4. Litmus
Mandatory before delivery. Five checks, all must pass. A draft that fails goes back to step 2, it does not go to the human.

    
5. Deliver
Draft to the clipboard or a file, character count noted for X. The human reads, edits on the fly, and posts. The skill never posts anything itself.

    
Anti-drift
If the draft "sounds smart", it failed. If any sentence could open a consulting deck, delete it. When unsure whether a phrase is him, grep the corpus. If its shape is not in there, it probably is not him.

  






  
08The litmus test

  
Runs on every draft. Scroll and watch it pass.

  

    
One breath per sentenceRead each sentence aloud. If it cannot be said in one breath, split it.

    
Zero hits on the ban listGrep for the banned words, emoji and em-dashes. Any hit means rewrite, not patch.

    
At least one direct questionTo the reader. A command wearing a question mark counts.

    
Every claim carries a figureNumbers beat adjectives. "45 minutes" beats "quick".

    
Ends on something practicalWhat he did, or what you should do. Never an abstraction.

11Make your own

  
Nothing here is specific to one person except the corpus. The method is the product.

  

    
Export your messages
Any chat log you own. Keep them verbatim, keep the typos, one delimiter between messages. A month is plenty. A thousand messages is enough to count on.

    
Run three analysts blind
Send the same forensic prompt to three different models. Do not let them see each other. Ask for quotes as evidence and numbered sections so you can line them up.

    
Keep only what counts
A rule goes in when all three agree and a grep over the full corpus gives it a number. Record the rejects too. Then point your assistant at the standard, every run, and make it pass the litmus before it hands you a draft.
